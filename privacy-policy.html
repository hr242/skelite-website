6. How we protect it

We take reasonable security safeguards appropriate to the data we hold, including:

  • access limited to those who need it, which in practice is a very small number of people
  • two-factor authentication on our email and file storage
  • encryption in transit, and encryption at rest provided by our storage platform
  • files delivered to buyers as Drive folders shared to one named email address, so access can be withdrawn, rather than as public links which cannot
  • no storage of payment credentials by us at any point
  • deletion of data at the end of the retention periods in section 4

No system is perfectly secure. If a personal data breach occurs that affects you, we will inform you and the Data Protection Board as required.

12. The law this is written to

Two regimes currently apply to personal data in India, and this policy is written to satisfy both.

The Information Technology Act 2000 and the SPDI Rules 2011 made under section 43A remain in force today.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 is law, and the DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 with an eighteen-month phased rollout. The Data Protection Board has been constituted. The Consent Manager framework applies from 13 November 2026, and the substantive obligations on notice, consent, retention, security and data principal rights are due by 13 May 2027.

So some of the duties described in this policy are not yet enforceable against us. We have adopted them anyway, for a practical reason: consent that was not taken cannot be applied retrospectively to data already collected, and a privacy notice issued this year will still be in circulation when the deadline arrives.
